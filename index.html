Swoop Shield · Ad blocker

Block ads and trackers on your whole Mac.

A free, system-wide ad blocker. Shield stops ads, trackers, telemetry and malware sites in every browser and app (Safari, Chrome, Arc, Brave, Edge, Firefox, even apps outside the browser) without slowing you down.

System-wide

Works through a macOS network extension, with a local resolver as fallback.

Uncloaks trackers

Catches trackers posing as a site's own subdomain (CNAME uncloaking).

No sneaking round

Stops apps bypassing it with their own encrypted DNS.

In-page ads too

Safari content blockers, plus an extension for Chrome, Arc, Brave, Edge and Firefox.

You're in charge

Pause for 5 minutes, allow a site, write custom rules, and see a blocked-today counter in the menu bar.

Stays on your Mac

All filtering happens locally. No telemetry.

DNS blocking can't remove ads served from the same domain as the video or page (like YouTube's). The browser extensions handle those on a best-effort basis.

Filter lists from EasyList, AdGuard, HaGeZi, OISD, Peter Lowe and URLhaus, downloaded from their maintainers.

FAQ

Good questions.

Is it really free?

Yes. Swoop is MIT-licensed, and there is no paid tier.

Is Swoop's cleaner like CleanMyMac?

Yes. It does the same job of freeing up disk space and removing files your Mac doesn't need (junk, caches, app leftovers, large and old files, duplicates) plus an uninstaller, login items and malware checks. The difference: it's completely free, open source, and every removal can be undone.

Is Shield an ad blocker?

Yes. Shield blocks ads, trackers and malware sites system-wide, for every browser and app on your Mac, not just one browser.

Which Macs does it run on?

macOS 13 Ventura or later, on Apple silicon and Intel.

Why isn't it on the Mac App Store?

The App Store sandbox would stop a launcher and cleaner from doing their job. Swoop is distributed as a DMG from GitHub instead.

What permissions does it need?

Accessibility, for window management, paste and snippets.

Full Disk Access, optionally, for the cleaner.

Shield's network extension, which you approve in System Settings › General › Login Items & Extensions.

Will the cleaner delete something important?

No. You review everything first, items go to the Trash by default, every removal is logged so you can restore it, and protected system paths such as /System are never touched.

Does Shield block YouTube ads?

DNS blocking can't remove ads served from the same domain as the video or page, as YouTube's are. The browser extensions handle those on a best-effort basis.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. There are no accounts and no telemetry, and everything stays on your Mac.

How do I open it the first time?

If macOS says it can't verify the developer, right-click Swoop in Applications and choose Open.
